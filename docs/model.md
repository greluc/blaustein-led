# The model

What the sets of Burg Blaustein contain that matters for the lighting: rooms, fires, candles,
windows, cavities for cables, and the places where the model comes apart. The findings come from
the product pages and the building instructions of BlueBrixx, read on 2026-11-19 and listed under
[The set](sources.md#the-set). Nobody who wrote this document has seen the model. Every statement
about a cavity or a joint is a reading of a drawing and is checked on the model before anything is
ordered or built in.

Only the instructions of the base castle and the Bergfried were read in full, as overview sheets
with the decisive pages enlarged. Those of the Vorburg and the Saalbau were read by helpers and a
few of their pages checked again; the others were sampled. Where an instruction was not read page
by page, a missing light point is not proof that there is none.

- The part counts are those printed on the cover of the instruction. For the Festungsspitzen, the
  Zehntscheune and the Hilfsschmiede the cover prints none; their counts come from one blog. Blogs
  give 5,494 parts for the Saalbau, 5,442 for the Münzturm and 4,605 for the Torhaus.
- The order of steps 1 to 9 is the one BlueBrixx states and the instructions follow. The product
  texts of the Hurde, the Münzturm and the Torhaus each require the sets before them; for the
  Schildwerk only its instruction shows that it builds on the Torhaus. The Festungsspitzen are
  optional.
- The side buildings stand free on their own plates and join the wooden yard through palisade
  sections with pins. The series also holds palisade sets, four packs of figures, carriages and a
  second line in a smaller brick format ("Mini Blocks", among them the 5,747 parts and the item
  110451 the earlier plan could not place).
- A display base for the complete castle is announced as set 110806: about 6,000 parts, 102 cm ×
  76.5 cm, more than 8 kg, for the castle up to the Schildwerk and the three side buildings. The
  Festungsspitzen do not fit on it. It is not in the shop yet. The one article that describes it
  calls its inside heavily braced with Technic beams and bricks, vertically and horizontally;
  whether a cavity remains for a control board is not published.
- No light kit exists for any of these sets, neither from BlueBrixx nor from the makers and
  marketplaces that were searched.

## What the castle stands on

The earlier plan placed the control board in "the hollow rock base". The instructions show
something else, and it changes with every stage.

| Stage | What is underneath | Cavities |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base castle | A square of 32 × 32 studs made of two layers of plates with one layer of bricks between them, on five round feet. Kitchen and courtyard stand on it. Three rock panels are pushed on sideways with Technic pins; the fourth side is the palisade | The space between the plates is 9.6 mm high. A chamber under the raised terrace is the only volume of useful height: roughly 80 mm × 80 mm plus an arm, 38 mm to 48 mm high, estimated from the drawings. It is reached by pulling a rock panel off its pins. A well shaft of 2 × 2 studs runs through the whole base to its underside |
| With Bergfried | The tower is built onto one corner of the same rock | The foundation of the tower lies across the rock at the corner of the chamber and probably locks the panel on that side |
| With Vorburg | A new, larger base of Technic frames and plates on eight feet. The whole base castle is set on top of it on its own feet; no pins or studs are drawn at the seats | The new base is again 9.6 mm high inside. Between the two bases a flat gap of about two plates remains, into which the well shaft opens. The base castle now stands in the middle of the model |
| With Hurde | A plinth under everything: a closed ring of rock sections joined by Technic pins, with a grid of Technic bricks inside and open fields between its beams. The castle is set on top | The largest cavity of the model. Its height and how the castle is held on it were not measured |
| With the display base | Not published; described as heavily braced with Technic beams and bricks | Unknown |

The owner's statement that the rock is hollow and reachable holds for the base castle alone, for
the chamber under the terrace. In the full build that chamber lies in the middle of the model
behind panels that are probably locked.

### What this means

- The sets contain fires, candles, flame bowls and two lanterns. No set has a torch holder, not even
  at a gate. Every torch of the lighting is an addition of a few original parts.
- The count of light points, with a torch at every gate, platform and wall walk, is 43 LEDs for the
  castle and about ten more for the side buildings. The two unclear points above are not counted.

## Where the model comes apart

Every joint a channel crosses needs a contact per channel and one for ground.

| Joint | How it is held |
| --- | --- |
| Base castle: roof, bedroom, hall | Loose. The floors end in tiles and locate each other by joists and a collar; no studs hold them. A connector has to tolerate a few millimetres of play and may not stand proud of the wall tops |
| Base castle: rock panels | Technic pins, pushed on sideways |
| Bergfried: roof, one wall strip of full height | Set on, and pushed on sideways |
| Vorburg: roofs, guard house on its columns, storeys of the watchtower | Set on as units |
| Base castle on the base of the Vorburg, castle on the plinth of the Hurde | Set on; how firmly was not established |
| Saalbau: roof, three wall panels of the hall | Set on. The product text says the wall folds open; the instruction shows panels that lift off |
| Münzturm: chamber, platform | Stacked modules |
| Torhaus: room with stair tower, sleeping floor, roofs, platform | Stacked modules |
| Side buildings | Free-standing; upper storeys and roofs lift off |

## Routes for cables

- **Well shaft of the base castle.** Two by two studs, from the courtyard through the base to its
  underside, and according to one article continued through the display base. The obvious way down
  for the trunk of the base castle.
- **Chamber under the terrace.** Open towards two arcades behind the rock panels.
- **Joist bays.** One brick deep under the bedroom floor of the base castle and under the hall floor
  of the Saalbau.
- **Tower shafts.** The watchtower of the Vorburg and the stair tower of the Torhaus are drawn as
  hollow shafts. The lower shaft of the Bergfried shows thick walls and only a small void.
- **Pillar.** The pillar in the cellar of the Saalbau is built from half-cylinder shells and appears
  hollow, about four studs across, with the tiled stove above it.
- **No flues.** The hoods of the fireplaces are solid; no chimney of the core sets is known to be
  hollow.

## Open points

- Every size of the chamber under the terrace, and whether its rock panels still come off once the
  Bergfried and the Vorburg are built.
- The height of the plinth of the Hurde and how the castle is held on it.
- The inside of the display base.
- Whether the wooden yard attaches again after the Schildwerk.
- Whether the flame pieces are transparent.
- The winch room of the Torhaus, the powder store of the Münzturm and three of the four flame pieces
  of the Torhaus were not located.
- What the sand-green block in the chamber of the Münzturm and the coloured tiles in the yard of the
  Torhaus are. Either may be a fire that deserves a channel.
- Whether the tower shafts and the pillar are open all the way.
- Which storeys of the Zehntscheune lift off.
